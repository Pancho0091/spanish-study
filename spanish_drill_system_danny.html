01 Review Read every word before picking up your pen
What is the Present Indicative?
The Presente de Indicativo is the tense of objective reality — facts, habits, and actions happening right now. In Mexican Spanish it also doubles as the future for confirmed plans: "Mañana trabajo desde casa" (Tomorrow I work from home) is grammatically a present tense but means a guaranteed future event.
The -AR Ending Sequence — memorize this order
Remove the -ar from the infinitive. What remains is the stem. Add these endings in order:

yo → -o  ·  tú → -as  ·  él/ella → -a  ·  nosotros → -amos  ·  ellos → -an

The one rule you cannot forget: The yo form of every regular -AR verb ends in -o. Always. No exceptions. trabajo · analizo · horneo · preparo · corro

Regular -ER and -IR Verbs

Almost identical to -AR. One difference only. Drill that difference until it's invisible.

Date
Started

01ReviewRead before drilling
-ER and -IR endings
-ER: yo -o · tú -es · él -e · nosotros -emos · ellos -en
-IR: yo -o · tú -es · él -e · nosotros -imos · ellos -en

The only difference between -ER and -IR: nosotros. -ER uses -emos. -IR uses -imos. Every other ending is identical. That's the entire lesson.
Still true from yesterday
The yo form still ends in -o. Always. For every regular verb regardless of -ar, -er, or -ir. como · corro · vivo · escribo

Week 1 · Day 3 · Present Indicative

Ser & Estar

Two verbs. Both mean "to be." Completely irregular — no stem to extract, no pattern to derive. These forms are memorized as fixed units, full stop.

Date
Started
Finished
01Review
SER — the blueprint
Use for permanent identity: what something fundamentally is. Origin, profession, character, material, time, relationships.

Soy analista de datos.  Adobe es una empresa de software.  La reunión es a las diez.
ESTAR — the condition
Use for temporary states: how something is right now. Location of people and moveable things, emotions, physical states, results of actions.

Estoy en la oficina.  Felix está recuperándose.  Los datos están listos.
The forms — fully irregular, memorize as fixed units
SER: soy · eres · es · somos · son
ESTAR: estoy · estás · está · estamos · están

Week 1 · Day 4 · Present Indicative

Ir, Tener, and Hacer

Three verbs you use every single day. Ir forms the near future. Tener and Hacer are yo-go verbs — their only irregularity is a -go ending in the yo form.

Date
Started
Finished
01Review
IR — fully irregular, memorize as fixed units
voy · vas · va · vamos · van

IR also builds the Near Future: voy a + infinitive = I'm going to [do something]. Voy a presentar los datos mañana. This is how Mexicans talk about future plans — always.
TENER and HACER — yo-go verbs
These are regular in every form except yo. The yo form ends in -go instead of the expected -o.

TENER: tengo · tienes · tiene · tenemos · tienen
HACER: hago · haces · hace · hacemos · hacen

TENER also has a stem change (ie): tienes, tiene, tienen — but not in yo or nosotros.

Week 1 · Day 5 · Present Indicative

Poder, Querer, Decir, Saber

The final core irregulars — and the modal rule that eliminates your most frequent production error.

Date
Started
Finished
01Review
The four verbs
PODER: puedo · puedes · puede · podemos · pueden (stem-change ue)
QUERER: quiero · quieres · quiere · queremos · quieren (stem-change ie)
DECIR: digo · dices · dice · decimos · dicen (yo-go + stem-change i)
SABER: sé · sabes · sabe · sabemos · saben (irregular yo only)
⚠ The modal rule — your most frequent error
PODER, QUERER, NECESITAR, DEBER connect directly to an infinitive. No preposition between them.

Puedo trabajar.  Quiero analizar.  Necesito comer.  Debo estudiar.
Puedo a trabajar.  Quiero a analizar.  Necesito a comer.

Exception: Voy a + infinitive is correct because the "a" belongs to IR, not to the following verb.
